About me

My interests lie in stochastic and decentralized optimization, machine learning, and their application to energy and healthcare systems.

I hold an M.S. in Industrial Engineering and Management with a concentration in Operations Research and Analytics, and I am completing an M.S. in Business Analytics and Data Science at Oklahoma State University, both with a 4.0 GPA. I currently work as an Advanced Analytics Intern with the Data Research and Analytics team at AAA - The Auto Club Group.

Before that, I was a Graduate Research Assistant in the Distributed Intelligent Systems (DISys) Laboratory, advised by Dr. Paritosh Ramanan, where I worked on decentralized optimization for decarbonizing energy-intensive industry. I continue that work by building an agentic AI framework that tunes the penalty parameter of decentralized optimization algorithms, so they converge efficiently without hand tuning.

Earlier, I spent three and a half years as a data analyst in industry and taught industrial engineering as a lecturer. I am now applying to PhD programs in Industrial Engineering and Operations Research for Fall 2027.

Research

The curiosity behind my research is a question I keep returning to: can independent systems, each guarding its own data, still decide together as well as if they had shared everything? Two directions of my work pursue it directly. Alongside them, I work on healthcare analytics, where the challenge shifts from coordination to extracting reliable signals from large, messy clinical data.

01

Decentralized decision-making under uncertainty

Power grids and the industrial plants they serve must plan together, but plants will not share their operating data. I build methods that let them coordinate anyway.

  1. Coordinate

    We developed a decentralized framework in which a 2,000-bus transmission grid and 26 electrified chemical plants schedule jointly through ADMM, exchanging only coordination signals instead of private operating data.

  2. Handle uncertainty

    My thesis adds uncertain on-site wind and solar. Each plant becomes a two-stage stochastic program, the grid and plants negotiate reserve capacity rather than power demand, and renewable scenarios are generated without pooling plant data.

  3. Forecast

    Federated learning carries the same principle into prediction. Plants train a shared load forecaster while their meter data stays local, so a newly connected plant with little history still receives accurate forecasts.

02

Agentic AI for decentralized optimization

In progress

Decentralized algorithms such as ADMM converge only as fast as their penalty parameter allows, and that parameter is usually tuned by hand for each problem.

I am developing skills for Scarlet Composer, our lab's multi-agent framework, that run the grid and each plant as independent agents solving their own subproblems. An LLM coordinator observes only privacy-preserving convergence signals and adapts the penalty, so coordination tunes itself across problems without any plant revealing its data.
